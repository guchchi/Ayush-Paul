I still remember standing in Delhi during winter mornings, feeling the heavy air around me. The fog was not just fog — it was smoke. The Air Quality Index crossed dangerous levels, and breathing itself felt difficult.

Like millions of people living in North India, I experienced the impact of stubble burning (Parali burning) every year. But instead of only complaining about pollution, one question stayed in my mind:

> Can a student like me actually help solve this problem?

That question became the beginning of my project — <strong>Stubble Solution</strong>.

## 🌍 The Problem That Needed Urgent Attention

Every year after harvesting crops like rice and wheat, farmers face a serious challenge. They must prepare fields quickly for the next crop. Farmers generally have two options:

<ul><li>Manually remove crop residue (time-consuming and expensive)</li><li>Burn the residue — the fastest but most harmful method</li></ul>

Most farmers choose burning because they lack time, machinery, or affordable alternatives. The result?

<ul><li>Massive smoke emissions</li><li>Dangerous PM2.5 particles</li><li>Toxic gases spreading across cities</li><li>Severe winter pollution in Delhi and Northern India</li><li>Respiratory problems for millions of people</li></ul>

This problem doesn't affect only India — it exists worldwide wherever large-scale farming happens. I realized this wasn’t just an agricultural issue. It was a health crisis.

## 💡 How I Got the Idea

Being from Delhi, I personally experienced pollution every winter. I saw elderly people struggling to breathe, children wearing masks, and news channels constantly discussing AQI emergencies.

Instead of blaming farmers, I tried to understand their reality. Farmers burn stubble because they need a fast solution. So my idea became clear:

👉 What if burning cannot be stopped immediately… but pollution from burning can be reduced drastically?

## 🏫 My Journey Began — School Level Exhibition

My journey started at <strong>Rajkiya Pratibha Vikas Vidyalaya Yamuna Vihar</strong>. Our guide teacher, Ashutosh Sharma, encouraged us to participate in a science exhibition supported by institutions like NCERT, SCERT Delhi, and NITI Aayog.

I worked on defining the problem, researching chemistry applications, and designing the concept. When our project secured 1st position at school level, it was the first moment I felt: Maybe this idea truly has potential. Our principal, Harish Kumar, motivated us to improve the model further.

## 🧪 Zonal Level — Facing Real Competition

The zonal competition brought together many reputed schools. Standing before judges and large audiences was intimidating. But when I explained the farmer challenges, chemical reaction logic, and environmental impact, the judges appreciated the practicality of the solution. Our project advanced again, building my confidence as a presenter and innovator.

## 🏆 District Level — Competing with Top Schools

The district round took place at GBSSS Surajmal Vihar. Competition level increased drastically. Students from elite private schools presented advanced models. Honestly, I felt nervous. But I focused on one thing: <strong>clarity over complexity</strong>. After intense questioning by judges, our project achieved First Position again. My teammate Samyak Jain and I travelled to Bhopal—my first long academic journey representing my school nationally. Presenting my innovation at the national stage was life-changing.

## ⚙️ Technical Explanation — How My Stubble Solution Works

My innovation combines chemical emission reduction and biological decomposition.

### Phase 1 — Pre-Treatment Spray

A decomposition solution containing ammonium sulfate, magnesium sulfate, calcium carbonate, phosphoric compounds, and enzyme boosters (cellulase & laccase). This softens crop residue and accelerates microbial breakdown.

### Phase 2 — Pollution Control Tablet

<ul><li><strong>Calcium Oxide</strong> — captures acidic gases</li><li><strong>Potassium Carbonate</strong> — catalytic oxidation</li><li><strong>Iron Oxide</strong> — improves combustion efficiency</li><li><strong>Ammonium Bicarbonate</strong> — reduces NOx emissions</li><li><strong>Magnesium Oxide</strong> — VOC breakdown</li><li><strong>Urea & Calcium Carbonate</strong> — neutralization reactions</li></ul>

👉 Result: Cleaner burning with drastically reduced toxic emissions.

### Application Process

<ol><li>Spray decomposition solution on stubble.</li><li>Allow overnight reaction.</li><li>Apply tablet powder before controlled burning.</li><li>Pollutants reduce significantly during combustion.</li></ol>
