# Outreach: Good vs. Bad

## Bad Outreach (What NOT to Do)

**Example 1 — The Generic Pitch**
> "Hi, I'm a freelance video editor. Do you need editing help? I'm available for work."

❌ Too generic, no personalization, sounds desperate.

**Example 2 — The Feature Dump**
> "I offer video editing, color grading, motion graphics, sound design, and thumbnail creation. I've worked with 5 clients and have 3 years of experience."

❌ All about you, no value to them, reads like a resume.

**Example 3 — The Lowball**
> "I can edit your videos for just $20. I'm trying to build my portfolio."

❌ Undercuts your value, signals desperation, attracts price-shoppers.

---

## Good Outreach (What to Do)

**Example 1 — The Observation (Editor)**
> "Hey Sarah — your travel content is stunning. I noticed your Reels cut off a bit abruptly at the end. I've been helping creators tighten their pacing so retention goes up. Happy to share a tip if useful."

✅ Specific compliment, specific observation, offers value first.

**Example 2 — The Compliment + Curiosity (Developer)**
> "Hey Mike — your product looks solid. I noticed the landing page takes about 4 seconds to load. Quick fix could boost your conversion rate. Do you handle that in-house or work with someone?"

✅ Specific observation about their situation, ends with a question, not a pitch.

**Example 3 — The Value First (Designer)**
> "Hey Priya — your new brand identity is clean. One small thing: your Instagram highlight covers don't match the style yet. Here's a quick mockup of how they could look consistent."

✅ Gives value upfront without asking for anything.

---

## The Difference

| Bad | Good |
|-----|------|
| Talks about yourself | Talks about them |
| Generic template | Specific observation |
| Asks for work | Offers value |
| Long paragraphs | 3 sentences max |
| No research visible | Clearly researched |
| "Can I help?" | Specific suggestion |
